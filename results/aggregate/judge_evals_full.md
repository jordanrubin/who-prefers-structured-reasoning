## subset 1, condition A, eval 1 (composite: 3.7)

**cross abstract inference: 4**

The synthesis frequently integrates findings across multiple abstracts to draw novel connections. For example, Section I weaves HS22 trend projections with HSEP94 smoker data, HSEP100 postmenopausal recurrence data, and HS38 CKD data to build a nuanced argument that technique selection is population-dependent. Section VIII links HS27 (GLP-1 and cubital tunnel), HS55 (weight-loss medications and DRF prevention), and HSEP124 (no effect on fracture union) to distinguish prevention from healing effects. However, a minority of claims remain single-abstract summaries (e.g., HS7 tendon rupture data, HS84 CBD trial).

**epistemic stratification: 4**

The synthesis consistently distinguishes between what the corpus shows and what it suggests. Phrases like 'if replicated, should prompt serious reconsideration' (HSEP100 recurrence), 'This argues for' (HS2 fat-soluble steroids), and 'If validated, this could fundamentally change' (HSEP43 ML prediction) demonstrate explicit epistemic layering. Section XII explicitly identifies gaps ('What Is Underrepresented'). However, the layering is not fully systematic throughout -- some sections present findings more flatly without flagging evidence strength, and there is no formal framework distinguishing evidence tiers.

**falsifiability yield: 3**

The synthesis generates some testable predictions: IMTN will become standard of care for metacarpal fractures within a few years (Section XIII), WALANT will become default anesthesia for common procedures, and the endoscopic CTR recurrence finding in postmenopausal women demands replication. However, most of Section XIII frames trajectories rather than specific falsifiable hypotheses with breaking conditions. The synthesis does not typically state what evidence would disprove its claims, which limits falsifiability yield.

**corpus coverage: 4**

The synthesis covers CTS, trigger finger, CMC arthritis, fracture management, nerve surgery, WALANT, disparities, pharmacologic influences, AI/technology, RA, xylazine, and cannabis -- representing the major topic clusters in a 218-abstract corpus. Section XII explicitly acknowledges underrepresented areas (tendon repair, wrist arthroscopy, congenital conditions, cost-effectiveness). The coverage is proportional to corpus density, with CTS appropriately receiving the most attention. Minor gaps include limited engagement with Dupuytren's beyond HSEP37/HS19, and some infection-focused abstracts (HS8, HS9) are not covered.

**assumption surfacing: 3**

The synthesis surfaces a few implicit field assumptions: that electrodiagnostic testing is necessary for CTS diagnosis (challenged by QCTS-6 and AI ultrasound), that injection frequency should be limited by rupture risk (challenged by HS7), and that 'double crush syndrome' requires multi-site decompression (challenged by HS23/LNS). Section XIII notes that database epidemiology has become dominant, implicitly questioning the assumption that this method captures nuanced outcomes. However, the synthesis does not systematically identify shared blind spots across the field, such as the assumption that short-term outcomes predict long-term success or that administrative database findings translate to clinical practice.

**decision readiness: 4**

Multiple sections provide specific actionable recommendations with conditions: standardize first-line trigger finger injection around triamcinolone/methylprednisolone (Section II), avoid NSAIDs in nonoperatively managed scaphoid fractures (Section IV), implement immediate mobilization after volar locked plating (Section IV), consider WALANT as default for routine hand cases (Section VI), and counsel CTS patients with anatomic variants to get preoperative ultrasound (Section I). The xylazine section recommends prioritizing substance cessation over complex reconstruction. These are specific enough for a hand surgeon to change practice, though some recommendations could benefit from more explicit patient-selection criteria.

**fabrication check:**

- The synthesis states 'Medicaid reimburses 14-17% less than Medicare' but the cited abstracts show a range of 11.3% (HSEP7, 2025 data) to 18.4% (HSEP7, 2016 data), with HS74 reporting 14.4%. The 14-17% range does not precisely match any single source, though it falls within the overall range reported.
- The synthesis states Medicare reimbursement decreased 29% from 2016-2025, which matches HSEP7 exactly ($108.86 to $77.33/wRVU). This claim is verified.
- The synthesis states HSEP100 shows 43.8% vs 12.7% recurrence rates. The abstract confirms these figures (oCTR 12.7% vs eCTR 43.8%). The direction is correctly attributed in the synthesis.

## subset 1, condition A, eval 2 (composite: 3.3)

**cross abstract inference: 4**

Strong cross-abstract integration is evident in several key passages. The WALANT section (VI) synthesizes HSEP65 (outcomes evidence), HSEP64 (environmental impact), HSEP102 (technological expansion), and HSEP97 (patient psychology) into a multi-dimensional argument, explicitly noting what the four abstracts collectively provide and what is still missing. The RA section (X) connects HS44's ACPA seropositivity paradox with HS53's TNF-alpha inhibitor finding to generate the novel inference that serologic profiling and medication optimization should be integrated into preoperative planning. Some individual abstract summaries within sections (e.g., HS68 AI therapy app, HSEP48 flexor tendon excision) stand alone without cross-referencing.

**epistemic stratification: 3**

The synthesis does distinguish evidence levels in places -- the HS84 CBD trial is correctly labeled 'preliminary results from 17 patients,' and HS30/HS40 are identified as rat models rather than clinical evidence. The HSEP100 recurrence data is flagged with 'if replicated.' However, many sections present retrospective database findings (TriNetX, PearlDiver) alongside prospective trials and RCTs without clearly differentiating their evidential weight. The corpus's heavy reliance on administrative databases is noted in Section XIII but this limitation is not applied retroactively to temper claims made in earlier sections that rely on exactly these databases.

**falsifiability yield: 3**

The synthesis offers several forward-looking predictions in Section XIII (IMTN replacing K-wires, WALANT as default, AI for bounded diagnostic tasks) but these are stated as trajectories without explicit falsification criteria. Some implicit testable claims exist: that HSEP100's 43.8% eCTR recurrence rate in postmenopausal women would replicate in a prospective study, or that HSEP43's ML model would validate externally. The synthesis does not generate novel hypotheses that go beyond what individual abstracts already state -- it mostly aggregates and extrapolates existing findings rather than proposing new testable mechanisms.

**corpus coverage: 4**

The synthesis addresses at least 13 major topic areas across its 13 sections, touching the majority of the 218 abstracts' subject matter. Coverage spans CTS (dominant), trigger finger, CMC arthritis, fractures (metacarpal and distal radius), nerve repair, WALANT, disparities, pharmacology, AI/technology, RA, and emerging substances. Section XII explicitly names underrepresented areas. The proportional representation appears reasonable -- CTS gets the most space matching its corpus dominance. Some smaller topics like lateral epicondylitis (HS16), Dupuytren's recurrence (HS19), UCL injuries (HS5), and pediatric conditions (HS31 mucopolysaccharidosis) receive minimal or no coverage, but these are appropriately minor in the corpus.

**assumption surfacing: 2**

The synthesis surfaces a few assumptions implicitly -- HS23's reframing of 'double crush syndrome' as 'longitudinal nerve sensitivity' challenges the assumption that proximal median nerve findings require proximal decompression. The observation that 'disparity awareness without disparity solutions' characterizes the field challenges the assumption that documenting inequities leads to remediation. However, the synthesis largely takes the corpus's methodological assumptions at face value: it does not question whether TriNetX coding accuracy can support the clinical conclusions drawn, whether propensity score matching fully addresses confounding in these studies, or whether the field's reliance on surrogate endpoints (reoperation rates, complication codes) reflects actual patient experience.

**decision readiness: 4**

The synthesis provides actionable guidance throughout: use fat-soluble steroids for trigger finger injection (Section II), consider prophylactic release in super-triggerer patients (Section II), avoid extended denervation for CMC OA (Section III), avoid splinting after volar locked plating of distal radius (Section IV), avoid NSAIDs perioperatively for nonoperative scaphoid management (Section IV), schedule DRF surgery at 6-15 days (Section IV citing HSEP137), and prioritize substance cessation over reconstruction for xylazine wounds (Section XI). These recommendations include clinical conditions under which they apply. The gap is the absence of explicit contra-indications or when-not-to guidance.

**fabrication check:**

- The synthesis states 'hand procedures generating roughly one-quarter fewer work-RVUs per operative hour than other plastic surgery procedures.' HS70 reports 6.23 vs 8.07 wRVU/h, which is approximately 23% fewer -- this is accurately characterized as 'roughly one-quarter.'
- The synthesis claims HS63 found 'lower adverse events and reoperation with IMTN versus K-wires (OR 0.52 and 0.23)' and 'greater total active motion at three months.' The abstract title references the comparison but the full text was not visible in detail. The claim is plausible but could not be fully verified against the truncated abstract.
- The synthesis states HSEP43 achieved 'AUC of 0.975 on the testing set with 100% sensitivity and 95% specificity.' The abstract confirms AUC 0.975, sensitivity 100%, and specificity 95% on the testing set. Verified.

## subset 1, condition A, eval 3 (composite: 3.2)

**cross abstract inference: 3**

While the synthesis is organized thematically and groups related abstracts, many paragraphs function as sequential summaries of individual abstracts rather than true cross-abstract inference. For instance, Section V presents HS30, HS40, HS42, and HSEP43 in series -- each paragraph summarizes one abstract's findings. The strongest cross-abstract inference occurs in Section I (CTS population-dependent technique selection across HS22/HSEP94/HSEP100/HS38) and Section X (combining HS44 and HS53 to propose integrated preoperative planning). But sections like II (trigger finger), V (nerve surgery), and IX (AI) read more as curated summaries with transitional sentences than as synthesized arguments where claims inherently require multiple sources.

**epistemic stratification: 3**

The synthesis uses some epistemic qualifiers -- 'if replicated' for HSEP100, 'preliminary results' for HS84, 'If validated' for HSEP43 -- showing awareness of evidence strength. Section XII identifies knowledge gaps. However, the three-level layering (shows/suggests/missing) is not consistently applied. Many findings from retrospective database studies are presented with the same confidence as prospective trials and RCTs. The synthesis does not explicitly distinguish between Level I evidence (HS46 RCT, HS68 RCT) and Level IV evidence (most TriNetX studies). The gap identification in Section XII is valuable but reads as a list of absent topics rather than an integrated assessment of where evidence is weakest within covered topics.

**falsifiability yield: 2**

The synthesis generates very few specific, testable predictions with breaking conditions. Section XIII lists six trajectories, but these are trend observations ('AI for bounded diagnostic tasks,' 'disparity awareness without disparity solutions') rather than hypotheses. A genuinely falsifiable claim would state something like 'If IMTN shows higher nonunion rates than plating in a prospective RCT of comminuted fractures, the biomechanical advantages demonstrated in HS71 do not translate clinically.' The closest the synthesis comes is the implicit prediction about HSEP100 recurrence rates demanding replication, but this is more of an observation about an existing finding than a novel hypothesis generated by the synthesis itself.

**corpus coverage: 5**

The synthesis achieves excellent breadth across the 218-abstract corpus. It addresses every major topic cluster: CTS (Section I, ~20 abstracts), trigger finger (Section II), CMC arthritis (Section III), fractures (Section IV), nerve surgery (Section V), WALANT (Section VI), disparities (Section VII), pharmacology (Section VIII), AI/technology (Section IX), RA (Section X), emerging substances (Section XI). Section XII explicitly catalogs underrepresented areas. The proportional emphasis matches corpus density -- CTS dominates both corpus and synthesis. Individual abstracts referenced span from HS2 to HSEP137, covering both podium and ePoster presentations. Even niche topics like xylazine wounds and environmental impact of surgical technique receive appropriate attention.

**assumption surfacing: 3**

The synthesis identifies several field-level assumptions: the belief that steroid injections cause tendon rupture (HS7 challenges this), that double crush syndrome requires dual decompression (HS23 challenges this), and that documenting disparities leads to solutions (Section XIII notes the gap between documentation and intervention). The observation that database epidemiology dominates research implicitly surfaces the assumption that administrative data captures clinical reality. However, deeper implicit assumptions go unaddressed: the assumption that statistical significance in propensity-matched retrospective studies approximates causal inference, the assumption that 90-day or 2-year endpoints capture clinically meaningful differences, and the assumption that single-institution findings generalize.

**decision readiness: 3**

The synthesis provides several practice-changing recommendations: use triamcinolone/methylprednisolone for trigger finger (Section II), reassure patients about tendon rupture risk (Section II), consider immediate mobilization after DRF plating (Section IV), and prefer WALANT for common hand procedures (Section VI). However, many recommendations are implicit rather than directly stated as action items. The synthesis describes what studies found but often stops short of explicitly saying 'clinicians should change X to Y when Z.' Section XIII identifies trajectories but does not translate them into specific decision rules. A hand surgeon reading this would learn a great deal but would need to extract their own action items from much of the text.

**fabrication check:**

- The synthesis claims HS55 shows 'OR 0.33-0.41 depending on drug class' for weight-loss medications and DRF risk. The abstract reports OR=0.38 overall, with subgroup ORs of 0.35 (intragastrointestinal), 0.41 (centrally-acting), and 0.33 (GLP-1 agonists). The 0.33-0.41 range is accurate.
- The synthesis states HS42 documents allograft use 'increased at a mean rate of +93.4 cases per year from 2017-2022, while autograft use declined (-1.4/year).' The abstract confirms: mean per-year rate of change for allograft was +93.4 and for autograft was -1.4. Verified.
- The synthesis states HSEP27 found 'wound care alone heals only 13%' and 'substance use cessation is the strongest predictor of healing (75% vs. 20.4%).' The abstract reports 13.0% (3/23) healing with wound care alone, and 75.0% (6/8) healing among those who stopped injecting vs 20.4% (9/44) among those who continued (p=.005). However, the 20.4% figure in the abstract represents those who fully healed among those who continued injecting, not specifically wound care alone patients. The synthesis may slightly conflate two comparisons, though the directional claim is accurate.

## subset 1, condition C, eval 1 (composite: 4.7)

**cross abstract inference: 5**

Nearly every claim integrates across multiple abstracts. The TriNetX monoculture analysis (Section I) weaves 25+ abstracts to identify infrastructure-shaped questioning. The technique equivalence landscape (Section II) synthesizes 12 comparison studies to reveal a corpus-level pattern invisible in any single abstract. The GLP-1 pharmacological surveillance section triangulates HS27, HS55, and HSEP124 to show a nuanced drug profile. The WALANT section synthesizes five distinct abstracts across different dimensions (cost, environment, patient experience, outcomes, procedure migration).

**epistemic stratification: 5**

Explicitly and consistently distinguishes between evidence, inference, and gaps. For HS51 (NSAID/scaphoid nonunion), it notes the association is 'real in the database but the causal inference is fragile' and explains confounding by indication. For HS44 (ACPA paradox), it states the finding 'demands mechanistic investigation' and notes unadjusted confounders. Section XII is an entire section dedicated to epistemic gaps. The WALANT caveat about nerve injury rates ('seems implausibly high and likely reflects coding artifacts') is sophisticated epistemic stratification within a single finding.

**falsifiability yield: 4**

Generates several concrete testable predictions: every new drug class will produce a hand surgery outcomes study within 12-18 months, IM nail fixation is displacing K-wires, nerve repair in 2030 will look meaningfully different. The WALANT section predicts the next research frontier is prospective cost-effectiveness analysis. However, some predictions are more trend observations than falsifiable hypotheses with specific breaking conditions. The piece could have generated more precise if-then predictions with measurable thresholds.

**corpus coverage: 5**

Covers all major topic clusters proportionally: technique comparisons, TriNetX methodology, WALANT, pharmacological surveillance, disparities, AI, pediatric hand surgery, biomechanics, reimbursement, nerve repair, conservative management, xylazine wounds, arthroplasty, and DRF. Explicitly references over 80 individual abstracts. Breadth across podium and ePoster presentations is balanced. Even niche topics like MPS-I (HS31, HSEP19), xylazine wounds (HS17, HSEP27), and tetraplegia WALANT (HSEP107) receive proportional attention.

**assumption surfacing: 5**

Identifies multiple field-wide blind spots: (1) TriNetX findings are hypothesis-generating not practice-changing; (2) technique equivalence obscures the real question of patient-specific selection; (3) the epistemological paradox of a surgical conference generating evidence against surgery; (4) biomechanical load-to-failure testing creates an 'illusion of clinical relevance'; (5) research production optimizes for publication rather than clinical truth. Each is a meta-level insight not stated in any individual abstract.

**decision readiness: 4**

Several recommendations are actionable with conditions: switch to fat-soluble CSI for trigger fingers (HS2, large effect, P<0.0001); do not limit CSI use based on tendon rupture fears (HS7); consider WALANT for pediatric procedures; avoid NSAIDs within 30 days of nonoperatively managed scaphoid fractures but not necessarily with operative management (HS69). However, the disparities section identifies problems without recommending interventions, and the reimbursement section notes decline without suggesting clinical responses.

**fabrication check:**

- HS25 synthesis claims 'combining nerve graft and transfer achieves superior shoulder abduction' which matches the corpus conclusion text, though the results section shows nerve transfer alone had higher AMS for abduction -- this blends the results and conclusion slightly differently but reflects the abstract's own stated conclusion.
- No clearly fabricated claims were identified across all verified references including HS2, HS7, HS12, HSEP65, HSEP64, HSEP118, HSEP102, HSEP97, HSEP129, HS44, HS60, HS68, HS77, HSEP43, HSEP135.

## subset 1, condition C, eval 2 (composite: 4.5)

**cross abstract inference: 5**

The synthesis is structured entirely around cross-abstract patterns rather than individual study summaries. The conservative-management evidence base (Section III) synthesizes HS7, HS12, HS16, HS4, HSEP116, and HSEP135 into a coherent narrative about a field producing evidence against its own primary intervention. **epistemic stratification: 4**

Strong epistemic layering throughout. The synthesis carefully labels TriNetX findings as 'hypothesis-generating, not practice-changing' and provides a worked example of the patient counseling conversation around HS51. HS44 is flagged as needing mechanistic investigation with explicit notation of unadjusted confounders. The HSEP65 nerve injury rate caveat shows awareness of coding artifacts. However, a few sections (e.g., Section IX on pediatric surgery, parts of Section XI on notable studies) present findings with less explicit epistemic framing, defaulting to summary rather than stratified interpretation.

**falsifiability yield: 4**

Section XIII generates eight numbered trajectories, several of which are testable: WALANT adoption expanding into new indications, IM nail displacing K-wires, AI entering a critical evaluation phase with the ratio of skeptical to optimistic studies increasing. The prediction that 'every major new drug class reaching widespread adoption will generate a hand surgery outcomes study within 12-18 months' is specific and falsifiable. However, the synthesis does not systematically formulate hypotheses with explicit breaking conditions or quantitative thresholds that would distinguish confirmation from disconfirmation.

**corpus coverage: 5**

Remarkably comprehensive. The 13 sections cover surgical technique comparisons, database methodology critique, conservative management, WALANT, pharmacological surveillance, reimbursement economics, disparities, AI/ML, pediatric hand surgery, biomechanics/cadaveric studies, notable individual studies, absences in the corpus, and future directions. **assumption surfacing: 5**

Exceptional at surfacing implicit assumptions. The most penetrating insight is that biomechanical precision ('load-to-failure in Newtons, stiffness in N/mm') creates an 'illusion of clinical relevance' -- identifying a methodological assumption the field does not question. The observation that reimbursement abstracts serve as 'a citeable evidence base for lobbying' surfaces a political assumption. The 'epistemological paradox' of surgical conferences producing conservative-management evidence challenges the field's identity assumption.

Provides actionable clinical guidance in several areas: HS2 recommends switching to fat-soluble CSI with a 30-point absolute difference in resolution rates; HS7 provides specific reassurance about tendon rupture risk (0.02-0.03%); the NSAID guidance from HS69 distinguishes acute vs chronic use; WALANT data from HSEP65 provides health-system-level rationale for adoption. The HS60 finding about three-dimensional deformity analysis is framed as directly challenging how surgeons evaluate distal radius alignment. However, the synthesis deliberately avoids oversimplifying into prescriptive bullet points for database-derived findings, which is epistemically appropriate but slightly reduces immediate decision-readiness.

- HSEP129 'ChatGPT correctly identified only 33.3% of hand and wrist fractures' -- corpus confirms 37/111 = 33.3%.
- No fabricated claims identified across all verified data points.

## subset 1, condition C, eval 3 (composite: 4.2)

**cross abstract inference: 4**

The DRF ecosystem (Section XIII-9) integrates 12+ abstracts across different angles on a single condition. The TriNetX critique provides a detailed worked example of why database associations should not be read as causal claims, complete with the specific clinical scenario of a patient asking about NSAIDs. The WALANT nerve injury rate caveat demonstrates that even favorable findings deserve scrutiny for coding artifacts. **falsifiability yield: 3**

The synthesis generates several directional predictions (8 numbered trajectories in Section XIII) but most are trend projections rather than falsifiable hypotheses with breaking conditions. Statements like 'WALANT adoption will expand' and 'AI will enter a critical evaluation phase' are plausible forecasts but lack specificity about what would disconfirm them. **assumption surfacing: 4**

These are strong meta-observations. However, some assumptions that could be surfaced are not: for example, the assumption that conference abstracts represent the field's research frontier (vs. what actually gets published), or the assumption that propensity-score matching adequately addresses confounding in observational database studies. The assumption surfacing is impressive but stops short of the deepest possible layer.

**decision readiness: 4**

**cross abstract inference: 4**

The synthesis consistently distinguishes evidence levels. It notes HS30 and HS40 are 'preclinical' and explicitly qualifies them as signaling a 'potential paradigm shift.' The HSEP43 machine learning model is conditioned on 'if prospectively validated.' The phrase 'overwhelmingly retrospective and database-driven' in the overview is itself an epistemic observation about the corpus's evidentiary weight. It does not, however, systematically use explicit labels like 'shows/suggests/missing' throughout.

**falsifiability yield: 3**

The synthesis generates some testable predictions: that WALANT will become the default for common hand procedures, that sutureless nerve repair may outperform sutured approaches, that GLP-1 neuroprotective effects deserve prospective investigation. **corpus coverage: 4**
